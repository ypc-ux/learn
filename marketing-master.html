04 · The Hook Library (A16Z Architecture)

Every hook: Context Lean-In → Scroll-Stop ("But / Here's the thing") → Contrarian Snapback → Value Transfer → Ease Close.

1 · Competitor Callout — "Your competitor booked the lead you missed."
2 · Time Bomb — "Contractors call back in 42 minutes. Homeowners hire by minute 10."
3 · Cost of Inaction — "Every week your website looks like 2015, you're paying for it."
4 · Insider Leak — "Roofers in Gwinnett book 20 appointments a month with this."
5 · Contrarian — "You don't need more leads. You need to answer the ones you have."
6 · The Enemy — "It's not your ads. It's your callback speed."
7 · Us vs Them — "Agencies charge $5K/month to do what this does for $297."
8 · Confession — "I built this because I got tired of watching good contractors lose to bad marketers."
9 · Question Filter — "What would 20 extra appointments a month do to your calendar?"
10 · Trojan Gift — "Here's the exact system, free. Steal it."

05 · The Trojan Horse Playbook

The mental model: The Greeks didn't breach Troy's walls in ten years of assault. They built a gift the Trojans valued, and the Trojans dragged it inside themselves. Your prospects have walls — ad blockers, spam filters, the scroll reflex. You don't break in. You build something they drag in.
  1. Extract a slice of the real paid service — valuable standalone, doable in under a week, needed by nearly all ICPs.
  2. Pitch only the gift. No payload in the first touch. The gift is the message.
  3. Overdeliver like it's the $10K project. This is the whole game.
  4. Let them ask "what else can you help with?" — the client who says that closes himself.
  5. The gift must be real. A fake gift reads as a pitch in camouflage. The walls go back up forever.

Sources: Bustamante's Trojan Horse Offer method ($10K first client, $100K+ total), Farnam Street mental model, Berger's Contagious (stories as vessels). Full chapter in the Web Design Bible.

The proven pitch: "Most contractors call leads back in 42 minutes. The average homeowner has hired someone else by minute 10. But here's the thing: we built a system that calls your leads in 5 minutes, pre-qualifies them, and books them on your calendar. One roofing company in Conyers got 20 booked appointments in 30 days. Paid $200 per appointment that showed up. Worth 15 minutes to see if it works for you?"

Rules: subject under 6 words · under 90 words total · one ask · no links in email 1 · Day 3 = one new fact (never "bumping this") · Day 7 = case study · stop at 3 touches.

06 · Copy Rules

  • Numbers over adjectives. "2,847 roofs" beats "decades of experience."
  • Second person. Their words for pain, never ours.
  • No AI slop: game-changer, seamless, unlock, elevate — all dead on arrival.
  • Read aloud. Wouldn't say it to their face → doesn't ship.
  • Before every deploy: the Copy Correctness Checklist in the Web Design Bible.
